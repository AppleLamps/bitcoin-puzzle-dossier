01 / PREMISE CHECK

Three facts change the case

What the primary record says before identity theories enter.

The familiar version of the story begins with a user called “Someone,” a thousand-bitcoin giveaway, and a January 15 announcement. None of those three details survives the source check.

1

“Someone” was a mistranslation, not an account

The Russian OP wrote кто-то — “someone” or “somebody” — about whoever launched the transaction. The actual poster was amaclin, who immediately wrote “it wasn’t me.” Verified

2

The first distribution was 32.896 BTC

The transaction spent one 32.9 BTC input into 256 outputs worth 0.001 × N BTC, with a 0.004 BTC fee. The roughly 1,000 BTC figure reflects later 2017 and 2023 additions. Verified

3

The announcement appeared January 23

The funding transaction confirmed January 15, 2015. The earliest public discussion found was the Russian-language Bitcointalk thread posted eight days later. Verified

02 / THE ANNOUNCEMENT

A discovery, not a confession

Bitcointalk topic 932434 · Russian local board

Legendary member amaclin posted “Забавная транзакция (Ханойские башни)” — “Funny transaction (Towers of Hanoi)” — at 07:43:22 forum time on January 23, 2015.

“Забавную транзакцию кто-то запузырил в сеть недавно … Сразу скажу — не я.”

“Somebody recently launched a funny transaction into the network … I’ll say right away — it wasn’t me.”
amaclin · translation from the Russian OPRead the original thread ↗

The thread supplied no creator biography, motive, or formal rules. Amaclin inferred the mechanism from the chain: output N appeared to use a private key in [2^(N−1), 2^N), making each next solution require roughly twice the computation. He published cracked keys 1–24 and cautioned that his own explanation was only a supposition.

What amaclin actually inferred

He described 256 outputs from 0.001 to 0.256 BTC, “clever” randomness, a key range doubling with each output, and a reward that increased without doubling. The “Towers of Hanoi” title and chessboard/rice-grain analogy framed the exponential search space. The thread received two replies on January 25 and then went quiet.

Why the English record starts much later

Bulista opened the English megathread on December 28, 2015 after finding the transaction “while playing around with my bot.” That thread — topic 1306983 ↗ — became the puzzle’s best-known public home. Bulista was a discoverer, not the creator.

03 / STRONGEST LEAD

The one-post claimant who could spend

A statement in April. A matching transaction in July.

On April 27, 2017, an account named saatoshi_rising made a single post: “I am the creator.” It described how the keys were made and promised to move outputs 161–256 into the unsolved lower ranges. Eleven weeks later, those exact outputs moved.

27 APR 2017

The claim

“I am the creator.” The account says there is no pattern and promises a redistribution.

11 WEEKS

The test

Only someone holding private keys 161–256 can execute the promised spend.

11 JUL 2017

The proof

All 96 outputs move on-chain into the lower unsolved puzzles.

Verified The post’s author controlled the puzzle keys. Likely The key holder was the original creator; a private transfer of the keys is possible in principle, but no evidence supports it.

“There is no pattern. It is just consecutive keys from a deterministic wallet (masked with leading 000...0001 to set difficulty). It is simply a crude measuring instrument, of the cracking strength of the community.”

Two proven actions, one likely one — and no personal revelation.

After the original funding, the public record contains one forum statement and three major on-chain interventions. Only spending from puzzle addresses proves possession of their keys.

06 / CLAIMS & THEORIES

What holds up — and what doesn’t

Evidence-ranked, not lore-ranked.

Early miner / whale

The source wallet’s scale and uncompressed-key predecessor support an early-holder profile. They do not identify a person.

Likely profile

Satoshi Nakamoto

The doubled “a” in saatoshi, double spaces, and “measuring instrument” language are wordplay and stylometry, not identity evidence.

Speculative

Polynomial key pattern

Fitted sequences and “2^n−1” theories overfit early solved keys. Key #4 equals 8, not 15; the key holder explicitly said there is no pattern.

Debunked

amaclin

He denied authorship in the first post, later described the author as unknown, and his own stated holdings conflict with the whale profile.

Not supported

Other “I am the creator” posts

Critical_Thoughts addressed the mystery rhetorically; albert0bsd joked about creating a software bug; Cricktor claimed to solve puzzle #66, not create the series.

Non-claims

A 2022 skeptic correctly argued that a bare forum claim proves nothing and asked for a signed message. No such forum signature appeared. But the July 2017 transaction answered the deeper question: the claimant could spend the puzzle outputs.

07 / OPEN QUESTIONS

Where the trail ends

Known gaps, preserved as gaps.

The research found no confirmed name, region, occupation, email, IP metadata, or personal history for the key holder. Timestamp and prose clues are too weak to narrow the field responsibly.
